# Thesis: NEAR is a bet that Intents revenue catches issuance

Generated by `python run.py` from `ledger/thesis_template.md`. Every number below is filled in from the data on each run. Data through {{as_of}} (UTC).

## The claim

NEAR pays for its security by minting new tokens. Holders get that dilution back in only two ways: gas fees that are burnt, and NEAR bought on the market with revenue captured from NEAR Intents.

Measured over the last 90 days, the two together return {{total_cover}} of issuance. Gas burn is {{burn_cover}} of issuance and will not close the gap. The Intents buyback is {{buyback_cover}} and is the only flow that can.

So the long-term case for holding NEAR reduces to one number: Intents volume has to reach about {{breakeven_volume}} a day at today's capture rate and price, which is {{breakeven_multiple}} times the current {{volume_day}} a day. Below that, holders are diluted. Above it, revenue from an application returns to holders everything that is minted to pay validators.

This is a conditional thesis. It says what has to happen, how far away it is, and what result would retire it.

## Evidence against

These cut against the thesis. They come from the same data.

- **The gap is large.** A {{breakeven_multiple}}-fold increase in volume is required with the capture rate unchanged. Volume is {{volume_yoy_change}} a year earlier and {{volume_qoq_change}} the previous 90 days, but cross-chain swap volume is cyclical and follows market activity, and one strong quarter is not a trend.
- **The capture rate is thin and exposed.** {{capture_rate}} of volume is a small margin in a business where routing is a commodity. Kill criterion K2 tracks it.
- **Price works against holders here.** Because the buyback is in dollars, a higher NEAR price lowers the share of issuance it offsets. The thesis cannot be "volume up, therefore price up" without limit.
- **Bought NEAR is held, not burnt.** The buyback lowers float, not `total_supply`. The header supply in this ledger does not fall because of it, and held tokens can be sold later. The buyback figure itself comes from DefiLlama's reading of NEAR's revenue accounts. This project checks it against the on-chain NEAR balance of those accounts (claim C15) but does not re-derive it trade by trade.
- **Base-layer usage does not pay.** Gas burn is {{burn_cover}} of issuance. Base-layer transactions averaged {{txns_day}} a day over 90 days against {{txns_day_365}} over 365 days. If Intents volume moved to another settlement layer, nothing on the base layer would replace it.
- **Stake is concentrated.** {{validators}} validators, and the {{nakamoto}} largest hold more than a third of stake; the ten largest hold {{top10_share}}.
- **Third-party inputs.** Price, transactions, active accounts, Intents volume, fees and revenue come from NearBlocks and DefiLlama. Only supply, issuance, burn, stake, staking yield and the revenue account balances are read from chain here.

## What would change the view

The thesis is retired if any of these holds for a 90-day window. `python run.py` evaluates them and prints the status; the current status is in [RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md).

- K1. Intents volume falls below the same 90 days a year earlier.
- K2. The captured revenue rate falls below 0.02% of volume.
- K3. Buyback revenue falls below 10% of issuance.
- K4. Realised issuance rises back above 3% a year.
- K5. The break-even multiple is higher than it was in the previous 90 days.
- K6. Fewer than 7 validators can hold a third of stake.

## What this does not claim

- It does not forecast a price.
- It does not claim the buyback will continue, or that its rules are fixed.
- It does not measure Intents volume from chain. That series is third-party and is labelled as such everywhere it appears.
- It is research, not investment advice.

## Method in one paragraph

The id of a NEAR epoch is the hash of the last block of the epoch two before it, so each block header points at the last block of the previous epoch. Following that pointer from the chain tip visits every epoch with one request each. Validator rewards are minted in the first block of an epoch, and inside an epoch supply only falls, by the fees burnt in each block. Reading `total_supply` at the first and last block of each epoch therefore gives minted and burnt NEAR exactly, with no indexer. The code is `ledger/epochs.py`; the identity is tested against the live chain in `tests/test_live_identity.py`.
